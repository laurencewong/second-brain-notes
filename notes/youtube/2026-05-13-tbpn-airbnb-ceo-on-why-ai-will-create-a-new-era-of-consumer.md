# Brian Chesky: Why Chatbots Are the Wrong Interface for Commerce

**Source:** [TBPN — "Airbnb CEO on Why AI Will Create a New Era of Consumer Products"](https://youtu.be/TyNufBceyZo), with hosts John Coogan and Jordi Hays interviewing Airbnb co-founder and CEO Brian Chesky.

Chesky on how Airbnb reaccelerated growth through "founder mode" intensity, why he thinks chatbot interfaces are structurally wrong for travel and e-commerce, and where he thinks rich-UI agentic commerce is headed next.

## Reacceleration through founder-mode intensity

Airbnb's revenue growth reportedly went from 10% to 18% year-over-year in one quarter. Chesky attributes this to a deliberate reset — a small team narrowly focused on conversion rate and the guest journey, operating with early-stage-startup intensity rather than public-company process. His broader claim: marketplaces decay by default without that kind of active intensity, and AI doesn't replace the need for founder presence — it raises the bar, because a competitor using AI well can now execute several times faster.

> "Marketplaces are really, really hard... it's kind of like gravity." — Brian Chesky

## AI inside Airbnb today

Chesky states that roughly 60% of Airbnb's code is now AI-written, but treats that as the less important number: customer-service cost per ticket is reportedly down about 10%, with 40% of contacts resolved by AI without a human handoff. His framing — code velocity is the visible AI win, but compressing the cost of a historically margin-killing function like customer service is the durable one.

## Leadership as presence, not absence

Chesky's stated leadership philosophy is that great leadership means staying close to the work, not delegating and disappearing — and he expects AI to intensify that requirement rather than relax it. As AI lowers the cost of execution, he argues the founder's remaining job (taste, prioritization, customer-experience calls) becomes more concentrated and more time-sensitive, the opposite of the common "AI reduces the need for hands-on leadership" assumption.

On competition, he frames Airbnb's app as a replicable asset and the real moat as the network itself — brand, two-sided liquidity, and habitual recognition — and says a small, hungry team (his example: "a different group of 20 or 30 year-olds") could rebuild the product layer if they wanted to.

## Why chatbots are structurally wrong for commerce

The core argument of the conversation: current chatbot interfaces are mismatched to travel and e-commerce for structural reasons, not just because the technology is immature.

1. **Text-based, photos as an afterthought** — travel and e-commerce are visual-first decisions; chat is a text channel with images bolted on.
2. **No direct manipulation** — every refinement requires typing a new prompt instead of applying filters or browsing in parallel.
3. **Hard to compare** — most commerce involves comparing many options, but chat collapses everything down to "here are the one or two best," which often isn't what the buyer wants.
4. **Single-player, not collaborative** — a large share of Airbnb bookings involve back-and-forth between multiple people, while chat interfaces assume one user in one context.
5. **Requires an account** — chat presumes identity persistence, while much of commerce starts with anonymous browsing.

Chesky's deeper claim is that these are architectural limits, not implementation bugs — even a flawless chatbot still caps the kind of workflow it can host, because the form factor itself (serial text, single-player, no spatial UI) is the constraint. As evidence, he points to third-party apps built on a major chat assistant's platform launching and then shutting down relatively quickly, arguing the text-first integration layer was too thin to support a real commerce surface.

> "I do not think a chatbot is the right interface for travel or e-commerce." — Brian Chesky

When a host describes what sounds like a richer version of chat-based trip planning — comparing hotels with photos and pricing, shareable with a travel partner — Chesky's response reframes the whole debate: that scenario requires AI agent *capability*, but delivered through a non-chat interface.

> "That's not a chatbot. It's going to be a completely different interface." — Brian Chesky

His positive thesis: the next generation of products will be agentic but richly visual rather than text-forward — closer to "an agent surfaces the right rich-UI view for what you're doing" than "add a chat window to an app."

## Consumer AI is underbuilt

Chesky cites a recent startup accelerator batch where only around 9% of companies (roughly 16 of 175) were consumer-focused, with the rest enterprise — a ratio he says hasn't moved in months. His read: AI tooling matures bottom-up because enterprise has clearer ROI and faster procurement, while consumer AI is harder because the right UI primitive hasn't been invented yet and distribution is brutally competitive. He sees that gap as the next big opportunity, not a reason to avoid the space.

## Other notes

- He calls CMO the highest-turnover executive role in Silicon Valley, arguing marketing has no durable playbook — what works shifts every few years, so the skill that survives is the ability to relearn quickly rather than defend an existing brand bible.
- Airbnb's growth bets beyond its core marketplace: hotels (a market he says is 8–9x larger than Airbnb's core), services ("there's no Amazon for services" outside a few verticals like rides and food delivery), and long-stay/nomadic living as remote work grows — each one reusing Airbnb's existing trust and supply network in an adjacent market.
- On marketing creativity: a Barbie-movie-themed listing stunt reportedly outperformed Airbnb's paid advertising, and he cites Red Bull's famous stratosphere skydive (under $1M spend, roughly a billion views) as an example of why differentiation — not repetition — is "the key to marketing," and why the discipline is to never "sequel" something that worked rather than repeat it.
