# Brian Chesky

Co-founder and CEO of Airbnb, trained as a designer at RISD. Publicly known for the Airbnb origin story (selling cereal boxes and renting air mattresses to make rent, being "ramen profitable," going through Y Combinator's W09 batch), guiding the company through a roughly 25% layoff during the 2020 pandemic followed by an IPO seven months later, and the 2022–2024 "Founder Mode" operating overhaul that reorganized Airbnb around direct CEO involvement in product and design.

## Founder Mode

In 2024, Chesky described publicly — at a Y Combinator founders' event, later written up by Paul Graham in an essay that popularized the term "Founder Mode" — an operating philosophy that pushes back on conventional management advice:

- Conventional wisdom says a CEO should hire great people, delegate fully, and avoid "micromanaging." Chesky calls this **manager mode** — well suited to running an already-mature company, but prone to organizational drift when applied to a founder-led one.
- In **founder mode**, the founder talks directly to the people doing the work (not only through layers of management), stays personally and continuously involved in product and design decisions rather than only approving work after the fact, and keeps functions like product, design, and marketing in a single functional structure rather than splitting them into independent divisions with their own general managers.

## The Airbnb reset (2022–2024)

As a concrete case study of Founder Mode in practice, Chesky:

- Dismantled Airbnb's divisional structure (general-manager-led business units) and rebuilt the company as one functional organization reporting to him.
- Took direct ownership of marketing, cutting a large amount of agency spend and reframing Airbnb's brand around designer-led campaigns ("Live Anywhere," "Categories," "Icons") rather than conventional performance marketing.
- Reinstated a twice-yearly "Summer Release / Winter Release" cadence — large, publicly dated product releases staged more like keynotes than routine feature drops.
- Brought in designer Jony Ive's firm, LoveFrom, as a design advisor.
- Cut a large number of features and projects, treating "deciding what to stop doing" as the first step of the reorganization, before any rebuilding began.

## Roadmap cadence

Chesky's release model: lock two dates a year roughly six months apart, give each release a single-sentence narrative focus early, and cut scope — never the date — to fit that narrative, then stage the release publicly, more like a product keynote than a changelog.

## Origin-story discipline

Chesky has told essentially the same Airbnb origin story — the air mattresses, the cereal boxes, being "ramen profitable," going through Y Combinator — consistently across more than fifteen years of interviews. The repetition looks deliberate: a memorable, specific, and falsifiable story travels and holds up better than a vague one.

## Career timeline

| Period | Focus |
|---|---|
| 2008–2012 | Founding Airbnb; scrappy early growth tactics; Y Combinator (W09) |
| 2013–2019 | Growth-stage CEO; built out a conventional divisional management structure |
| 2020 | Pandemic hit Airbnb's business hard; ~25% layoff; recovery culminated in a December 2020 IPO |
| 2022–2024 | "Founder Mode" reorganization: functional structure, direct CEO product ownership, Summer/Winter release cadence |
| 2024– | Public advocate for Founder Mode as a broader management philosophy |
